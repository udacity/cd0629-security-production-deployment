# Effective Udacity Projects

An effective Udacity project should:

- Cover all of the required learning objectives for the course.
- Take the average learner about 4-6 hours to complete a standard practitioner project or 1.5-2 hours for a business leader project.
- Provide instructions that require the learner to use their critical thinking skills.
- Allow the learner to apply the job-ready skills the gained in the course content to solve a new real-world problem.
- Produce a portfolio-ready deliverable that the learner can share with pride.

> 💡 Challenge the learner -- but don't overwhelm them.

## Project Design Standards

Effective project design ensures that projects are **engaging, skills-focused, and aligned with industry expectations** to prepare learners for real-world applications of their knowledge.

### Details

- **Projects reflect real-world scenarios and industry relevance.**
  - Projects are designed around authentic, industry-relevant challenges that align with job tasks.
  - Problem-based tasks encourage inquiry, decision-making, and cross-disciplinary thinking.
  - Projects simulate professional environments, requiring learners to apply technical skills in realistic contexts.
  - Scenarios are inclusive and relevant to our globally diverse audience.
- **Projects are hands-on and experiential.**
  - Learners engage in active, “learning-by-doing” experiences through interactive elements such as coding environments, labs, or simulations.
  - Projects result in concrete artifacts (e.g., code repositories, data analysis reports, ML models, security audits) that demonstrate practical application of skills.
  - The project structure supports problem-solving, critical thinking, and independent learning.
- **Projects develop job-ready competencies using industry-standard tools and workflows.**
  - Tasks and challenges align with the skills and responsibilities of specific roles
  - The tools, frameworks, and methodologies used in projects mirror those currently in professional settings (e.g., version control, cloud platforms, automation tools).

## Project Instructions Standards

A good set of project instructions is essential to setting students up for success. Project instructions should tell the student what to build, how to build it, and why the project is relevant to their career aspirations.
Our goal is to find the sweet spot between “recipe-book” instructions that provide so much detail that students don’t have to think about what they are doing and “treasure hunt” instructions that provide too little detail and force the students to reach out for help to figure out where to start. A project that is too challenging can discourage students from completing it and finishing the course.
Clear and complete project instructions improve project pass rates and graduation rates.

### Why Are Project Instructions Important?

Clear project instructions help learners focus on applying their skills, not on figuring out what they’re supposed to do. When instructions align with rubric requirements and are easy to follow, learners are more likely to complete the project successfully and confidently.
Clarity reduces confusion, minimizes unnecessary support requests, and ensures a smoother experience for both learners and reviewers.

### **Details**

- **Instructions must clearly reflect all submission requirements**.
  - Every requirement should be addressed directly or embedded in a clearly described task.
  - Instructions and rubric requirements are in sync, and there are no expectations in one that aren’t reflected in the other.
- **Instructions are structured for clarity and ease of use.**
  - Steps are concise and logically ordered.
  - Lengthy instructions are broken into manageable sections.
  - Multi-part projects are organized across separate pages when needed.
- **Students can track progress with clearly defined tasks.**
  - Task lists are included to outline key steps and completion milestones.
  - Task items align with project objectives and expected deliverables.
- **Setup requirements are fully documented.**
  - All necessary tools, dependencies, and configurations are listed with clear references.
  - Starter code, templates, and datasets are described and linked.
- **Instructions include checkpoints to verify progress.**
  - Coding projects include automated checks (e.g., unit tests) to validate correct setup and implementation.
  - Non-coding projects include structured self-checks to confirm progress.
- **Submission expectations are unambiguous.**
  - The required submission format, including allowed file types and file names (where applicable), is explicitly stated.
  - A final checklist ensures students include all necessary components in their submission.

Learners should not encounter surprises during review if they’ve followed the instructions carefully.
